<p>NOTE: DRAFT OUTLINE</p>

<p>I had the opportunity to spend the month of January travelling Japan to visit my partner's family in Nagasaki. We flew from Phoenix to Tokyo, and on return from Nagasaki to Prescott. That means we also had an opportunity to visit Tokyo and Ghibli attractions (Nagoya) on our way to Nagasaki.</p>

<h2>Preparations</h2>

<p>Travel guidance will always inform you to travel light as public transit options will be crowded and if you want to avoid many fees for carrying excess luggage, it is best to just have a couple carry-on size bags with you. So I travelled with only a few outfits. In order to ensure that we could visit the Ghibli attractions, museum and park, we had to prepare two months in advance. Tickets are competitive but by logging in early and working together, we were able to get our tickets. I also attempted best I could to scope out vegan options to eat ahead of time, because otherwise, it can be a struggle. I utilized the Happy Cow app as well as a list created by a vegan social media account to locate vegan options.  </p>

<h2>Departing Flight</h2>
<p>We travelled using Hawaiian airlines, a leg from Phoneix to Honolulu, then Honolulu to Tokyo. I have to admit, I may have been a little miserable on my flight, which was the longest flight I have been on. So I don't really have a lot of guidance on how to avoid misery, other than, I did not know that the little flaps on the head of the seat can move to cradle your head if you want to sleep, and that would have been good to know ahead of time. However, I did manage to enjoy watching Zootopia and Batman vs. Yakuza. It is really important for vegans to call ahead of time to arrange your food, although you are not always guaranteed to have an option. I failed to arrange my meal for the flight there, but on the returning flight, I did, and I was very happy with it. More on that later.</p>

<h2>Exploring Tokyo</h2>

<p>We slept off our jetlag in the Tokyo Airport Hotel. We walked around the city, seeing Godzilla, the Government building light show and overlook, and the big screen cat advertisement.</p>

<h2>Shinkansen and visits to Ghibli attractions</h2>
  <p>As my partner and I are both fans of the Ghibli franchise, we were excited to visit the attractions.</p>

  <h3>Shinkansen</h3>
  <p>The trip to Nagoya did take us past Mt. Fuji, but it was too cloudy to see it. Nevertheless, the view of the countryside was nice to see. </p>

<h3>Museum</h3>
  <p>The museum was a testimony to Miyasaki's love of traditional animation and his caution about the direction the industry is taking.</p>

<h3>Park</h3>
<p>The park is an immersive journey through Miyasaki's fantasy world, giving participants the opportunity to put themselves in the films. </p>

<h2>Nagasaki</h2>
<p>We spent the majority of our time in Nagasaki city, and I felt fortunate to get an indepth experience of the city. </p>

<h3>Nagasaki City</h3>
<p>Due to being a port city, Nagasaki has been a hot spot for cultural fusion since its opening to the outside world. Its shipyard is also why it was targeted for the atomic bomb. </p>

<h4>Grandma's Apartment</h4>
<p>Grandma is a survivor of the atomic bomb and so the Japanese government subsidizes a nice apartment for her in the city. She enjoys a great view of the city and easy access to shops and services. She always has chambara dramas on the TV. I particularly enjoyed when an all female group did a musical version of chambara. I also got to enjoy the NHK New Years special.  We slept on futons in a tatanmi room, and enjoyed the Japanese bathroom experience. We would have a vegan version of traditional japanese breakfast each morning, which included rice, tofu, pickles, natto, miso soup, and a tangerine. For dinner, we often got vegan bentos from the downstairs grocery store. </p>

<h4>Peace Park and Museum</h4>
Because grandma is a survivor of the atomic bomb, we went to the Peace Park and Museum. It is a moving exhibit. I am against all war, not just atomic weapons, and hope that one day we will see the end of them.

<h4>Megane Bridge</h4>
<p>Megane Bridge or "Spectacles Bridge" is known for having a reflection that looks like glasses and is also a romantic destination due to a heart shaped brick.</p>

<h4>Hamanamachi Arcade</h4>
<p>Hamanamachi Arcade is a popular mall where you can shop. </p>

<h4>Nagasaki Station</h4>
<p>Nagasaki Station also has a popular mall where you can shop and a place where you can get the biggest variety of vegan bentos, so we visited a lot.</p>

<h4>Dejima</h4>
<p>Dejima is a place where you can learn about the cultural exchange between the dutch and japanese. </p>

<h4>Shrines</h4>
<p>There is a prominant shrine with many stairs that many Japanese people go to. There is also a cool ceramic shrine nearby. My favorite shrine however, is the Neko Shrine, where cats are celebrated. I got my fortune there. </p>

<h4>Nagasaki Observatory</h4>
<p>Although its called an "observatory" which we often think of in English as a place to look at stars, in this case it is a place to look at the city. We visited in the day and night in order to get both views. </p>

<h4>Glover Garden</h4>
<p></p>Glover Garden is a place where you can learn about the cultural exchange between the scottish and japanese. </p>

<h4>Tea</h4>
<p>We visited a couple locations to drink matcha. </p>

<h3>Unzen</h3>
<p>A little outside the city is a national park where you can explore a very active geyser. There are also many cats there.</p>

<h2>Returning Flight</h2>
<p>Relatives drove us to Nagasaki Airport where we again took Hawaiian Airport home. Nagasaki to Honolulu, and then Honolulu to LAX, and LAX to Prescott. These flights were a little less miserable. </p>

<h2>Concluding Thoughts</h2>
<p>I never could have guessed in advance how much I would learn about Japanese history and culture from the trip. I am immensely grateful for my opportunity to visit. </p>
